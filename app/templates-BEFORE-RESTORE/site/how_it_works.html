How it works

This is the actual sequence of stages every run goes through — each one reads the previous stage's output and writes its own, so the whole pipeline can be re-run, audited, or debugged stage by stage.

1

Data sources

Raw Sentinel-2 satellite imagery, ESA WorldCover land-cover data, and administrative boundary data are validated for integrity before anything else runs.

2

Acquisition & scheduling

A region and a year-pair to compare are selected, and every required dataset is re-confirmed valid before processing begins.

3

Satellite image processing

Cloudy pixels are masked out, then NDVI (a vegetation-health measure) is computed for each year from the raw imagery, and the two years are differenced.

4

Vegetation loss detection

Pixels where NDVI dropped beyond a defined threshold are flagged as genuine loss — filtering out normal seasonal fluctuation.

5

Polygon generation & analysis

Flagged pixels are grouped into real mapped areas, each with an accurate hectare size (via proper map projection) and a severity level.

6

Forest boundary check

Each area is checked against real land-cover data to classify it as inside protected forest, outside it, or unclassifiable due to missing coverage.

7

Alert generation

Inside-forest and outside-forest loss areas each become a formal alert record, with slightly different information suited to each audience.

8

Evidence generation inside-forest only

Each inside-forest alert gets a PDF report, structured metadata, its exact polygon shape, and a true-color satellite snapshot of the affected area.

9

Blockchain integrity layer inside-forest only

Every evidence package is fingerprinted and chained, block by block, so any later change to the evidence becomes detectable on re-verification.

10

Government & NGO dashboards

The Forest Department sees the full picture — map, evidence, blockchain status, analytics. NGOs see a separate awareness-focused view of outside-forest loss, by design, without evidence or blockchain features.

Why "outside forest" doesn't get evidence or blockchain: those alerts are for awareness and community action, not enforcement — the extra verification overhead isn't needed for that purpose, by design.
